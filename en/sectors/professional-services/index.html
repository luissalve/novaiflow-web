Where this can help your operation

  1. Finding a precedent takes too long

    Connect an assistant to an agreed collection of business documents. Answers can reference source material, while access rules limit what each team member can retrieve.

  2. Client deliverables repeat the same preparation

    Structure intake information, assemble draft sections and check required fields. A reviewer remains responsible for the final interpretation and the material delivered to the client.

  3. Reporting depends on manual consolidation

    Bring data from approved sources into a repeatable reporting workflow. Separate source facts, calculated values and generated commentary so reviewers can assess the output.

Connected to your existing systems

We review document repositories, project systems, client records and reporting sources. Document ownership, access permissions and revision history determine which material is suitable for retrieval or generation.

A practical starting point

Choose one recurring deliverable or internal knowledge task. Define an evaluation set with your team, including questions that should not be answered from the available sources. Review factual support, completeness and the effort needed to finish the work.

Questions before starting

Can an assistant respect different client access permissions?

Permissions must be designed into retrieval and tool access. We assess the source systems and test separation between users and client workspaces before rollout.

Can it explain where an answer came from?

Source references can be part of the workflow when the underlying material supports them. We also define how the assistant responds when information is missing or conflicting.

Can AI send final work directly to clients?

Delivery permissions are agreed during scoping. For reviewed professional work, a practical starting point is draft preparation followed by approval from the responsible person.
