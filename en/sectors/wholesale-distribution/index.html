Where this can help your operation

  1. Orders must be re-entered by hand

    Extract order details from agreed document formats, match customer references to your catalog and prepare records for review. Unknown products, quantities and pricing exceptions go to the responsible team.

  2. Availability differs between systems

    Define which system owns stock and how updates reach sales, purchasing and fulfillment. Track failed updates and reconciliation differences instead of letting them accumulate silently.

  3. Purchasing decisions require assembling reports

    Bring sales history, current inventory and supplier information into a shared view. Agreed rules can highlight items for review; purchasing commitments follow your approval process.

Connected to your existing systems

Typical starting points include order management, ERP, inventory or warehouse software, accounting and supplier files. We assess product identifiers, units, customer terms and synchronization frequency before connecting the workflow.

A practical starting point

Begin with a defined order channel or product range. Test product matching, partial fulfillment, price differences and returns alongside straightforward orders. Assign an owner to exceptions and compare the result with the current process.

Measures to agree before implementation

  • Manual entry time per order
  • Orders requiring product or pricing corrections
  • Unresolved inventory synchronization differences

Questions before starting

Can this work with our current ERP?

We assess available access, data structures and vendor limitations first. The scope depends on what can be read and updated reliably in your existing system.

Will AI place purchase orders on its own?

Purchasing permissions and approvals are part of the design. The first workflow can prepare recommendations or draft orders for a buyer to review.

What if our catalog data is inconsistent?

Product mapping and data cleanup may be prerequisites. We identify those gaps during discovery and include the necessary work in the implementation scope.
